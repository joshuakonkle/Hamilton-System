# Bots

Four seats · not a starter pack

The same four seats as Become an Operator. This page shows a public reference for each seat. It is not five things to buy, and you do not stand all four up at once.

## 1. Primary Bot

Start here when you want help turning a task into finished work.

Grok Bot is an SI teammate that finishes real work by signing into your tools, using them like you, and returning completed tasks. Bots can research, draft, and collaborate in parallel while learning from the workflows and context you give them.

[x.ai/bot](https://x.ai/bot)

## 2. Scaffold Bot

When you need a bot for a specific job, this helps you define its responsibilities and set it up.

Dr. EggBot designs high-quality Grok Bots by asking preference questions and using CreateAgent. It creates coding bots with one job, unslopped and verified, and non-coding bots with one job, one voice, explicit anti-jobs, and no leftover tools.

[Dr. EggBot](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/dr-eggbot-v2)

## 3. PM/QA Bot

When several bots are working together, this helps keep work coordinated and checks that the results meet your requirements.

The engineering guide describes a fleet with a head of operations who manages onboarding, daily meetings, postmortems, and alignment, plus engineer bots that review code and verify work. No seat approves its own work.

[Grok Bot for engineering](https://x.ai/bot/guides/grok-bot-for-engineering)

## 4. Working Bot

Tony, the PERM Watch Bot. Runs a weekly PERM labor-market test for a qualified U.S. job seeker. It applies to PERM ads, flags employers who stay silent past 24 hours, drafts the silence post, preps regulator filings, with you approving every send.

## 5. Useful bots get onboarded like new hires {#how-our-bots-onboard}

A bot with access to the right systems isn't ready to do a job yet. The SpaceXSI accounting team put it plainly:

"Bots need the same investment in documentation, onboarding, and ongoing coaching as new employees."

[Building an accounting team with Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-accounting), Annay (Paul) Bains, Will Smerdon and Erin Jenks

That's how we run our own SI team. Each bot holds one seat with one scoped job, and its seat description is its operating manual, changed only with the process owner's approval. A bot drafts, a QA seat checks, and only a person's own go sends, spends or publishes. New seats shadow the work and propose before they run it.

We go one step further than the source. A process people can run reliably may still need realigning before a bot can run it. More important, putting a bot on a process can show that the human process was wrong, or could never have succeeded. That's the lesson at the heart of our consulting and training.

A client process we're redesigning now

At one client, several people send contacts to one person who enters them into the business development system. Incomplete submissions and that person's other demands made entries inconsistent. Putting a bot on it exposed that friction: it asks for missing details right away and writes straight to the system, not through the screen, so we're changing the process with the bot as a human extension.

Learn how bots are built and given repeatable work: [Create and manage Bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots) and [Skills and routines](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations).
