# American System Historical Baseline

A practical reading and reference resource for understanding the Hamiltonian / American System through its key historical figures and original sources.

This document underpins the five-module webinar series and the America First Policy Tracker. It is designed to help business leaders, local officials, and citizens build a clear mental model of productive sovereignty, directed credit for real production, innovation over labor arbitrage, and selective high-capability human capital.

## Core Mental Model: Four Pillars

When assessing historical figures, policies, or modern proposals, filter them through these four pillars:

1. **Sovereignty** — National and local control over critical economic, monetary, technological, and human-capital levers.
2. **Production** — Credit, policy, and incentives directed toward real physical production and capacity rather than pure financialization or inflationary activity.
3. **Innovation over Labor Arbitrage** — Preference for genuine technological and process innovation that raises productivity and reduces costs, rather than temporary foreign labor or pure cost-arbitrage models.
4. **Selective High-Capability Immigration + Repatriation** — True high-capability innovators who advance next-level American capability may stay or immigrate. People who do not contribute to sovereignty, production, or innovation should return to their home countries (where they can help build similar systems if allowed).

These pillars provide a consistent lens for both historical study and modern local application.

## Recommended Digestion Sequence

1. Start with the short primary sources (Clay and Lincoln). Read them through the four pillars.
2. Move to the focused secondary books on Clay’s System and McKinley’s protectionism.
3. Add supporting biographies and the Jackson counter-view as needed.
4. Return to primary sources periodically to stay grounded in original language.

## Primary Sources (Start Here)

### Henry Clay
- **“In Defense of the American System”** (Senate speech, February 2, 3, and 6, 1832)  
  The single most important primary document.  
  [Official U.S. Senate page with PDF](https://www.senate.gov/artandhistory/history/common/generic/Speeches_ClayAmericanSystem.htm)

### Abraham Lincoln
- Collection of his tariff and American System statements (including early platform language affirming the national bank, internal improvements, and high protective tariff).  
  [Abraham Lincoln’s Classroom – Tariff collection](https://www.abrahamlincolnsclassroom.org/abraham-lincoln-in-depth/abraham-lincoln-and-the-tariff/)

### William McKinley
- Tariff-related speeches and statements (McKinley Tariff of 1890 and later defenses).  
  Entry point: [Library of Congress – William McKinley Papers](https://www.loc.gov/collections/william-mckinley-papers/)

## Secondary Sources (Prioritized)

### Highest Priority – Directly on the System
1. *Henry Clay and the American System* – Maurice G. Baxter  
   Most targeted treatment of Clay’s economic program (tariffs, national bank, internal improvements).

2. *William McKinley, Apostle of Protectionism* – Quentin R. Skrabec Jr.  
   Best focused examination of McKinley’s tariff philosophy and its industrial results.

### Strong Supporting Biographies
3. *Henry Clay: Statesman for the Union* – Robert V. Remini  
4. *William McKinley and His America* – H. Wayne Morgan  
5. *Andrew Jackson and the Bank War* – Robert V. Remini (essential for understanding the opposition)  
6. *Lincolnomics: How President Lincoln Constructed the American Century* – John F. Wasik  

### Partial Continuity
- *The Rise of Theodore Roosevelt* – Edmund Morris  
- *Power and Responsibility: The Life and Times of Theodore Roosevelt* – William Harbaugh  

## Practical Application Notes

Use these sources to:
- Ground local workforce, manufacturing, and infrastructure conversations in historical precedent.
- Explain why directed credit for production differs from pure financialization.
- Illustrate the long American tradition of preferring domestic productive capacity and genuine innovation over dependency or arbitrage.
- Equip business and civic leaders with clear language for sovereignty-focused strategy.

Modern examples (such as deliberate replacement of non-sovereign CDL drivers with American veterans) are contemporary expressions of the same principles.
