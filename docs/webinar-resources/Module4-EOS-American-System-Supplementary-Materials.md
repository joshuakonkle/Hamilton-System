## 1. Diagnostic Checklist

Use with leadership or alone. Prefer facts over aspirations. Complete in the **guided checklist** (Yes / Partially / No + short note). No essay required.

### Process as documented and followed
- [ ] Core processes are identified, documented simply, and actually followed — not only posted once and ignored.
- [ ] Level 10 uses process clarity when something breaks (not only “try harder” or “hire faster”).

### Elevation vs. pure elimination
- [ ] Process improvement aims to **elevate work** into higher-skill design, maintenance, or system roles — not only to cut hours or headcount.
- [ ] Team can state the **Elevator logic**: when the dedicated operator role disappeared, value moved into design, controls, and upkeep — not into “cheaper operators forever.”

### Failure mode (process that rents weakness)
- [ ] **Failure mode check:** No core process is designed so that critical steps only work with permanent short-cycle temporary or visa labor coverage.
- [ ] Where short-cycle temporary or visa labor appears, it is labeled **bridge**, not permanent process design.

### One-line hand-offs
- [ ] At least one process fix points at a capacity metric (Module 3) or a seat family (Module 5) — this companion does not redesign Scorecard or People.
- [ ] Process Rocks (if any) stay owned in the Rocks system (Module 2).

### Anchors
- [ ] Team can state: we either design processes that build lasting capability or we design processes that depend on rented capacity.
- [ ] **Posture:** Operating in the United States is not workforce-neutral. Seat design either builds lasting productive capacity or rents weakness.

**Diagnostic result (one line):**  
Biggest gap — undocumented process, improvement that only cuts hours, critical steps that depend on permanent short-cycle temporary or visa labor coverage, or Level 10 that never uses process in IDS?

## 2. 90-Day Outline (Free Execution Scaffold)

This is a **starting outline**, not a full managed engagement. Paid implementation support (e.g. 90-Day Sovereign Capacity Sprint) sits behind deeper facilitation and cross-module integration.

### Days 1–30 — Diagnose
| Action | Output |
|--------|--------|
| Name 1–2 core processes that touch critical delivery or knowledge | Process list |
| Map where steps depend on short-cycle temporary or visa labor coverage | Bridge vs permanent labels |
| **Failure mode:** Flag any critical step that only works as permanent short-cycle temporary or visa labor design | Explicit risk list |
| Note whether recent “improvements” elevated skill or only removed hours | Elevation vs cut notes |

### Days 31–60 — Redesign
| Action | Output |
|--------|--------|
| Document one core process simply (followable, not a binder) | One-page process |
| Redesign one step so work elevates (skill, knowledge retention) rather than only shrinks | Before/after step |
| Remove or time-box any permanent short-cycle temporary or visa labor assumption on a critical step | Design choice noted |
| One-line link to a capacity metric or seat family | Hand-off note only |

### Days 61–90 — Execute & Embed
| Action | Output |
|--------|--------|
| Run the documented process in live work; IDS breaks in Level 10 | Follow-through notes |
| Keep the elevated step and bridge-only short-cycle temporary or visa labor coverage for next quarter | Locked process choice |
| Hand off measurement to Scorecard (Module 3) and seats to People (Module 5) | Short hand-off list |

**Success check at day 90:**  
One core process is documented and followed; at least one step elevates work rather than only cutting hours; no critical step remains on unchallenged permanent short-cycle temporary or visa labor design — **so process builds lasting productive capacity rather than rents weakness.**
