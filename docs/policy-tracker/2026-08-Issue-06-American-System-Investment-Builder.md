## Why this tracker exists

The other five trackers score policy: science and capacity, nuclear and quantum, the Federal Reserve, hemisphere security, sovereign workforce. Operators still ask a money question: *if I want savings pointed at American productive strength, what does that look like next to a broad index?*

Most index products do not ask whether the company makes anything here, or whether standing seats are a citizen pipeline or short-cycle temporary or visa labor used as a standing bench. This tracker does. It publishes a growing screen and a **paper** sleeve so the filter is visible — not so anyone is told to trade it.

Screen surface: [investments.digitalknowledge.net](https://investments.digitalknowledge.net)

**Plain-English terms**
- **Screen** — a list of issuers that pass a written filter. Not a fund. Not a recommendation. Primary and secondary filters below are how a name *gets on the list*. They are not the same as the chips on the investments table.
- **Paper sleeve** — a $10,000 equal-weight *illustration* vs SPYM (the same core holding used in the Trump Account default), started **29 July 2026**. When a name is marked Green IN, it joins Sleeve Y. Equal-weight restates across current Sleeve Y **from that original start**. Not a recarve on the add day. Not simulated buys or sells. Numbers compare *ideas*. They are not an instruction to purchase. Live weights, the name list, and the returns snapshot live at [investments.digitalknowledge.net](https://investments.digitalknowledge.net).
- **SPYM** — a broad U.S. equity market fund used here only as the benchmark line. We do not treat SPY and SPYM as interchangeable on this page.
- **Green** — on the list **and** in the paper sleeve (Sleeve Y).
- **Green+Watch** — on the list with one conversion question open. **Sleeve Y or Sleeve N is a separate chip.**
- **Red** — on the list as American *capability* (plants, tools, or lines that matter here) but **not in the paper sleeve**. Weight = $0 until the overlay converts. **Why this name is Red is in that row’s Watch note.** Every Red row must have a Watch note. No Watch note, no Red on the public table.
- **Primary filter** — does this issuer operate U.S. productive capacity we care about (yards, mills, enrichment, reactors, launch, 300-millimeter semiconductor lines, tools into those lines)? Pass this gate or the name is not on the list.
- **Secondary filter** — after elevation: visa stack and whether U.S. operations are workforce-aligned. Operating in the United States is not workforce-neutral. Fail this gate and the name can stay on the list as Red.
- **Visa stack (observed)** — public Department of Labor filings we can count: H-1B Labor Condition Applications (the form an employer files before hiring on an H-1B temporary work visa), H-2B / H-2A seasonal and temporary-worker petitions under the named employer and known subsidiaries, and PERM (the labor-certification step toward a green card). Missing data is **Unconfirmed**, not zero.
- **Floor labor (unobserved / observed)** — people on the plant, mill, yard, scrap, or contractor floor whose status is not in the visa-stack filings: Temporary Protected Status (a time-limited humanitarian work authorization), Employment Authorization Documents (work cards issued for several non-immigrant categories), people without lawful status, and contractor crews the issuer does not petition for directly. Employers almost never publish this. **Unobserved** unless a filing, raid, 10-K, or union record says otherwise. Unobserved is not, by itself, a Red flip.
- **Global Capability Center (GCC)** — a standing design, software, or manufacturing book outside the United States used as *capacity*, not as a sales office. A GCC as the model for core seats is why a firm can have U.S. plants and still fail the secondary filter (the Microsoft-class pattern).
- **Capacity class** — one closed label for *what this issuer actually makes or launches on U.S. soil* (naval yard, enrichment, launch / space, and so on). We use a class so the table can be filtered by kind of capacity, not by a paragraph of marketing. The story behind the class sits in notes, not in the chip.
