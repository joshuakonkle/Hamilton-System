### 2. Definitions (plain English)

- **Screen** — a list of issuers that pass a written filter. Not a fund. Not a recommendation. Primary and secondary filters below are how a name *gets on the list*. They are not the same as the chips on the investments table.
- **Paper sleeve** — a $10,000 equal-weight *illustration* vs SPYM (the same core holding used in the Trump Account default). Numbers compare *ideas*. They are not an instruction to purchase. Live weights and the name list live at [investments.digitalknowledge.net](https://investments.digitalknowledge.net).
- **SPYM** — a broad U.S. equity market fund used here only as the benchmark line. We do not treat SPY and SPYM as interchangeable on this page.
- **Green** — on the list **and** in the paper sleeve.
- **Green+Watch** — still Green (in the paper sleeve), but **floor labor** is still **unobserved**. Floor labor is defined below: plant, mill, yard, and contractor seats that do not show up in H-1B / H-2B filing sets. Unobserved does not mean clean. It means we do not have a public count.
- **Red** — on the list as American *capability* (plants, tools, or lines that matter here) but **not in the paper sleeve**. Weight = $0 until the overlay converts. **Why this name is Red is in that row’s Watch note.** Every Red row must have a Watch note. No Watch note, no Red on the public table.
- **Primary filter** — does this issuer operate U.S. productive capacity we care about (yards, mills, enrichment, reactors, launch, 300-millimeter semiconductor lines, tools into those lines)? Pass this gate or the name is not on the list.
- **Secondary filter** — after elevation: visa stack and whether U.S. operations are workforce-aligned. Operating in the United States is not workforce-neutral. Fail this gate and the name can stay on the list as Red.
- **Visa stack (observed)** — public Department of Labor filings we can count: H-1B Labor Condition Applications (the form an employer files before hiring on an H-1B temporary work visa), H-2B / H-2A seasonal and temporary-worker petitions under the named employer and known subsidiaries, and PERM (the labor-certification step toward a green card). Missing data is **Unconfirmed**, not zero.
- **Floor labor (unobserved / observed)** — people on the plant, mill, yard, scrap, or contractor floor whose status is not in the visa-stack filings: Temporary Protected Status (a time-limited humanitarian work authorization), Employment Authorization Documents (work cards issued for several non-immigrant categories), people without lawful status, and contractor crews the issuer does not petition for directly. Employers almost never publish this. **Unobserved** unless a filing, raid, 10-K, or union record says otherwise. Unobserved is not, by itself, a Red flip. This is the term **Green+Watch** points at.
- **Global Capability Center (GCC)** — a standing design, software, or manufacturing book outside the United States used as *capacity*, not as a sales office. A GCC as the model for core seats is why a firm can have U.S. plants and still fail the secondary filter (the Microsoft-class pattern).
- **Capacity class** — one closed label for *what this issuer actually makes or launches on U.S. soil* (naval yard, enrichment, launch / space, and so on). We use a class so the table can be filtered by kind of capacity, not by a paragraph of marketing. The story behind the class sits in notes, not in the chip.

**Lock:** every public Red row carries a Watch note. Research owns the fill. Presentation does not invent it.

### 3. Key policy and operational signals

**Why a screen now.** Issues 1–5 already describe an administration that talks capacity, nuclear, yards, launch, and citizen seats. Equity tape often ran ahead of plants coming online. This page does not chase that tape. It asks whether the *issuer* still fits the filter after the run-up and the giveback.

**Two gates, not a vibe.**  
1. Elevate only if there is U.S. productive capacity in a class we named.  
2. Put it in the paper sleeve only if the visa stack and Global Capability Center test do not show short-cycle temporary or visa labor as the standing model for core seats.

**Visa stack is the Issue 5 pairing on a ticker.** Naval yards and launch pads with export-control / clearance seats tend to pass gate two. Analog design benches and Global Capability Centers with hundreds of H-1B Labor Condition Applications in a year fail gate two and stay **Red** — still listed, because the *capacity* is real. The Watch note on that row is the public why.

**Local pairing (Texas first, then Indiana).** Sinton sheet (Steel Dynamics), Starbase / Bastrop / Terafab-area build (SpaceX), Sherman 300-millimeter lines (Texas Instruments, Red until design seats convert — see that row’s Watch note). Fort Wayne / Butler / OmniSource scrap-to-steel is the Indiana pairing. Local pairing means plants and citizen pipelines under the federal signal — not a ticker tip.

### 5. Hamiltonian / productive-sovereignty readout

Hamilton’s system was never “buy the market.” It was credit and attention pointed at *what the country must be able to make*. Clay and Lincoln ran the same idea through tariffs and internal improvements. This page is a public version of that filter applied to listed companies: plants and yards first, then whether the seats under those plants are a citizen base or a rented bench.

Red is not a morality play and not a short thesis. Texas Instruments still pours 300-millimeter wafers in Texas. Caterpillar still pours iron and builds power modules here. Applied Materials still sells the tools those lines buy. They stay on the list because the *capacity* is American System-relevant. They stay out of the paper sleeve because the observed visa stack and, where present, the Global Capability Center say U.S. operations are not workforce-aligned *yet*. Conversion would be a scorecard event — execution, not a press release.

### 6. Implications for business and local leaders (next 6–12 months)

1. **Read Red as a workforce design problem**, not as “bad stock.” If your firm looks like TXN/CAT/AMAT on visas and a Global Capability Center, you will not pass this screen. That is the point of Issue 5 pairing with this page.
2. **Name the plant, not the ticker.** Sinton, Starbase, Butler, Newport News, Portsmouth enrichment, Lynchburg naval nuclear — those are local pairing sites. Workforce boards and schools attach to sites.
3. **Do not confuse tape with throughput.** Several of these names already had an optimistic run when the administration named the sector. Plants still take years to show up in revenue. This page tracks the filter, not an entry (long/short).
4. **Paper first.** If you use the sleeve at all, use it as a comparison vs SPYM on paper. Personal rules, lot management, and Class A/B structure stay off this tracker.
5. **Watch conversion, do not narrate it early.** GDIT aligning to the yard core, Jewett/Canada moving, Applied Materials winning 300-millimeter tool packages into U.S. mega-fabs (including the Terafab-area build) instead of staying Asia-weighted — those are future scorecard lines when they happen.
