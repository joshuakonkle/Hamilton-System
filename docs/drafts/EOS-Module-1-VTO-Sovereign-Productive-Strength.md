### 1. Why Vision Is the Constraint

Every EOS tool ultimately rests on people — that was the kingpin logic of Module 5.  
Those people tools only compound when the Vision/Traction Organizer points at the right future.

If the 10-Year Target and 3-Year Picture still assume continuous access to low-cost temporary labor, or treat growth as pure financial scale without domestic productive strength, then Rocks, Scorecard, and Level 10 Meetings will chase the wrong target. They will optimize cost and speed-to-fill instead of capability.

The American System starts from a different premise: national and company strength rest on real productive capacity, high-skill American roles, and control of critical work — not on permanent labor arbitrage or asset narratives alone.

This module shows how to run the existing V/TO so the long-range picture demands sovereign capacity. It does not invent new EOS components. It applies a Hamiltonian filter to the ones you already use.

### 2. EOS Tools Through a Hamiltonian Lens

**10-Year Target**  
Default risk: revenue, headcount, and market share only; labor model and location of critical work left implicit.  
Hamiltonian filter: Does the target explicitly include domestic productive strength and high-skill American roles as part of success — not as an afterthought? If hitting the target exactly as written would leave you *more* dependent on short-cycle non-sovereign labor, the target is incomplete.

**3-Year Picture**  
Default risk: “more of the same, scaled.”  
Hamiltonian filter: What capacity, skills, and supply-chain control must exist in three years so the 10-Year Target is sovereign rather than rented? Name the critical seat families and the capability you must own.

**1-Year Plan**  
Default risk: activity and fill-rate goals.  
Hamiltonian filter: At least one annual priority that builds pipeline or capability in a critical seat family — language that can become an M5-style capability Rock next quarter.

**Core Focus / Niche**  
Default risk: market positioning only.  
Hamiltonian filter: What will you own and control versus what will you rent? Innovation over labor arbitrage belongs in identity, not only in a later cost-cutting Rock.

**Marketing Strategy / 3 Uniques**  
Default risk: brand claims disconnected from real capacity.  
Hamiltonian filter: Where relevant, proof points tied to domestic capability and American skill — not claims that assume permanent external arbitrage.

**Vision-level Issues List**  
Default risk: generic obstacles.  
Hamiltonian filter: Issues that block sovereign capacity — skills pipelines, credit for productive investment, control of critical supply or logistics nodes (see also Policy Tracker Issue 4 on hemisphere security as precondition).

### 3. Practical Contrast

**Common pattern**  
V/TO is filled with revenue, headcount, and market goals. The labor model and knowledge retention are invisible. Downstream Rocks optimize cost and speed-to-fill. Institutional knowledge is treated as replaceable.

**American System pattern**  
V/TO makes productive sovereignty visible. The 10-Year Target and 3-Year Picture require high-skill American capacity and domestic control of critical work. Rocks and Scorecard (Module 5 and later modules) inherit that filter. Success includes capability growth, not only scale.

The elevator logic from Module 5 still applies: process innovation should elevate work into higher-skill American roles, not only eliminate hours. The long-range picture should demand that elevation.

### 4. Concrete Working Questions

Use these in a leadership session or as a self-serve pass over the current V/TO:

1. If we hit our 10-Year Target exactly as written, would we be more dependent on short-cycle non-sovereign labor — or less?  
2. Does the 3-Year Picture name the critical seat families and capability we must own?  
3. Where does the current V/TO still assume “we can always hire or contract our way out of a skill gap”?  
4. What one line in the 10-Year Target or 3-Year Picture would force next quarter’s Rocks to build capacity instead of only cutting cost?  
5. Does Core Focus still treat labor arbitrage as a permanent advantage, or as a temporary bridge while capability is built?

---

### 5. 90-Day Execution Path Inside an EOS Company

**Days 1–30 – Diagnose**  
- Pull the current V/TO with the leadership team.  
- Run the working questions above.  
- Mark every place the long-range picture assumes temp dependence or pure financialized growth without domestic capacity.  
- Identify one critical seat family whose future is invisible in the 3-Year Picture.

**Days 31–60 – Redesign**  
- Rewrite 10-Year Target and 3-Year Picture so domestic productive strength and high-skill American roles are explicit.  
- Align at least one 1-Year priority with an M5-style capability Rock (pipeline, retention of knowledge, or sovereign seat design).  
- Update Core Focus language where it still assumes permanent arbitrage.

**Days 61–90 – Execute & Embed**  
- Socialize the updated V/TO in a Level 10 or quarterly session.  
- IDS one vision-level issue that blocks sovereign capacity.  
- Hand off one concrete seat-family implication to the Module 5 workforce discipline.  
- Confirm Scorecard leading indicators (from M5) still match the new picture.
