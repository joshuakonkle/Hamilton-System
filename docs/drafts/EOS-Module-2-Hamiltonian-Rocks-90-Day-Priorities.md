### 1. Why Rocks Are the Execution Constraint

Every EOS company already knows the rule: Vision without Traction is a wish list. Rocks are the 90-day priorities that force focus and ownership.

What most companies still miss is *what those Rocks are optimizing for*.

If the quarter’s Rocks only cut cost or fill seats faster, the company can hit every Rock and still become more dependent on short-cycle temporary and visa labor. Institutional knowledge keeps walking out the door. Capability does not compound.

Module 5 established the people filter: high-skill American workers as the foundation of productive capacity, not a pure cost center. Module 1 put that filter into the V/TO — the 10-Year Target and 3-Year Picture must demand sovereign capacity. Module 2 makes the next 90 days obey that picture.

This module does not invent new EOS tools. It applies a Hamiltonian filter to Rocks, ownership, To-Dos, and IDS so Traction builds capacity instead of only renting hours.

**Cross-reference**

BMC and EOS are complementary, not conflicting: same American System filter, different operating system. ### 2. EOS Tools Through a Hamiltonian Lens

**Quarterly Rocks**  
Default risk: cost cuts, fill-rate, and activity volume only.  
Hamiltonian filter: At least some Rocks explicitly build pipeline, knowledge retention, or high-skill American seats in a critical family. Cost discipline stays real; it is not the only scoreboard.

**Rock ownership**  
Default risk: owner of a number (spend, headcount, cycle time) with no capability outcome.  
Hamiltonian filter: Owner accountable for a capability result — e.g., qualified internal candidates for a critical seat family, retention of institutional knowledge, or a working pipeline step — not only a cost or fill metric.

**Rock wording**  
Default risk: “Reduce temporary visa worker spend 15%.”  
Hamiltonian filter: “Stand up internal pipeline that produces X qualified American technicians for critical seats” (or equivalent). The first may still be useful as a supporting discipline; the second is what compounds.

**To-Dos (7-day)**  
Default risk: busywork under a weak Rock.  
Hamiltonian filter: Steps that move the capability Rock — sourcing, training design, mentor assignment, Scorecard metric definition — not only administrative noise.

**IDS on stalled Rocks**  
Default risk: blame, reset, or drop the Rock without diagnosis.  
Hamiltonian filter: IDS whether the Rock was cost-shaped vs. capacity-shaped. If it was only a cost Rock, redesign it so the next 90 days build something durable.

**Link to V/TO**  
Default risk: Rocks disconnected from the 3-Year Picture.  
Hamiltonian filter: Every major Rock should be traceable to a line in the updated V/TO from Module 1. If it cannot be traced, it is probably optimizing the wrong target.

### 3. Practical Contrast

**Common pattern**  
Rocks optimize labor cost and time-to-fill. Capability is assumed to follow. Temporary and visa labor remain the default bridge for every skill gap. When the Rock hits, the spreadsheet looks better and the company’s productive depth is unchanged or weaker.

**American System pattern**  
A critical subset of Rocks builds sovereign capacity. Cost control remains; it is paired with pipeline and knowledge retention. Hitting the Rock leaves the company with more high-skill American capability than it started with — not only a lower contractor line.

The elevator logic from Module 5 still applies: process and priority design should elevate work into higher-skill American roles, not only eliminate hours. A Rock that only removes spend without building a seat or a pipeline is incomplete under this filter.

### 4. Concrete Working Questions

Use these in a quarterly Rocks session or as a self-serve pass:

1. Of this quarter’s Rocks, how many build capability in a critical seat family — versus only cut cost or increase activity?  
2. If every Rock hit 100%, would dependence on short-cycle temporary or visa labor go down, stay flat, or go up?  
3. Which Rock is owned as a number only, with no clear capability outcome?  
4. What one Rock rewrite would force next quarter’s Scorecard to track pipeline health (Module 5 language)?  
5. Does any Rock still assume “we can always contract or visa our way through the gap”?

### 5. 90-Day Execution Path Inside an EOS Company

**Days 1–30 – Diagnose**  
- List current Rocks with the leadership team.  
- Tag each as cost/fill vs. capability.  
- Align the list with the updated V/TO (Module 1) and the critical seat family from Module 5.  
- Identify one Rock that is pure cost language and one seat family that has no capability Rock.

**Days 31–60 – Redesign**  
- Rewrite 1–2 Rocks into explicit capability Rocks (pipeline, knowledge retention, or high-skill seat design).  
- Assign owners accountable for the capability outcome, not only the spend number.  
- Add matching Scorecard leading indicators in Module 5 language (pipeline health, retention of institutional knowledge, share of critical seats held by long-term American workers).  
- Confirm each major Rock traces to a line in the 3-Year Picture.

**Days 61–90 – Execute & Embed**  
- Run Level 10 Meetings that IDS stalled capability Rocks — diagnose shape (cost vs. capacity), not only personality.  
- Capture what blocked pipeline or knowledge retention.  
- Decide the next seat family and the next Rock set so the filter continues next quarter.  
- Hand one concrete implication back to the Module 5 workforce discipline if seats or GWC need a second pass.
